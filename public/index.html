            The method, in plain words

            • To temper (the tadka): heat 1 tbsp mustard oil in the open pot, then add 1 scant tbsp from and ¾ tsp from , in the order the recipe says. The spices fry briefly in the hot oil and flavour the whole dish.
            • Low flame means the smallest flame your burner can make, a quiet blue flicker. Trust the minutes more than the whistle count, because cookers differ.
            • Cabbage family (cabbage, cauliflower, radish, turnip): chop first, leave for 10 minutes, then cook lightly. Never cook them under pressure.
            • On top, raw: olive oil, ground flaxseed and lemon go on after cooking, with gooseberry in season. Heat would spoil them.
            • Never fill the cooker more than half with lentils and water. They foam and swell, and a too-full cooker can block the vent.

            Weigh once, then use the same bowl

            Recipes say “1 small bowl” or “3 big handfuls”. To make that exact, weigh each ingredient once on a kitchen scale, notice where it comes to in your own bowl, and use that bowl from then on.

            A small steel bowl holds about 150 ml, but they differ. A level tablespoon is flat across the top; a heaped one has a mound. A handful is what fits in your cupped palm; a big handful is what you can grab with your fingers spread. Greens shrink a lot when cooked, which is why the amounts look big.

            Kitchen basics

            Using a pressure cooker safely
            • Before you start: check the rubber ring is sitting flat inside the lid, and the little vent under the weight is clear (hold it up to the light).
            • Fill it: never more than half full with food and water together.
            • Close it: line up the lid, turn until it locks, and place the weight on the vent.
            • First whistle: the loud hiss when the weight lifts. Then turn the flame down as the recipe says.
            • When the time is up: switch the flame off and wait. The weight settles flat when the pressure is gone. Never lift the weight or run the cooker under the tap to speed it up.
            • Open it tilting the lid away from your face, because the steam is hot.
            Rinsing and soaking lentils and beans
            • Rinse: put them in a bowl, add water, rub them gently with your fingers, and pour the cloudy water away. Repeat until the water runs mostly clear, usually 3 times.
            • Soak: cover with plenty of water, because they swell to about twice their size. Cover the bowl and leave it for the time in the recipe.
            • Hot weather: keep long soaks in the fridge so they do not turn sour.
            • Afterwards: drain and rinse again before cooking.
            Washing vegetables and greens
            • Vegetables: hold each one under running water and rub the skin with your hands for a few seconds. Do not just dip them.
            • Greens: fill a big bowl with water, swish the leaves around, and lift them out. Do not pour the water through them, or the sand goes back on. Change the water and repeat 3 times. Take extra care in the monsoon.
            • Then dry them: shake the leaves and spread them on a clean towel. Wet greens spoil quickly.
            Chopping an onion, ginger and garlic
            • Keep your fingers safe: curl the fingers of the hand that holds the food, like a claw, so your knuckles guide the knife and your fingertips stay tucked away. Cut slowly. Put a damp cloth under the board so it does not slide.
            • Onion: cut it in half through the root, peel off the skin, lay the flat side down, and slice. Then turn the slices and cut again into small pieces.
            • Garlic: press each clove with the flat side of the knife until the skin splits, peel it, and chop it small.
            • Ginger: scrape the skin off with the edge of a spoon, then slice thin and chop small.
            Heating mustard oil, and what to do if it flares
            • Heat it until it first smokes: put the oil in the open pot on medium flame and watch it. When a thin wisp of smoke first rises, turn the flame down low. Raw mustard oil tastes harsh; heating it this way mellows it.
            • Do not walk away. Oil can go from smoking to burning in under a minute.
            • If it catches fire: turn the flame off and put a lid over the pot. Never pour water on burning oil.
            • Never reuse mustard oil that has been heated to smoke.
            Keeping leftovers safely
            • The second bowl: let it cool for a few minutes, move it to a glass container, and put it in the fridge within an hour.
            • Reheat: until it is steaming all the way through, not just warm at the edges. Add the toppings only after reheating.
            • Fish and chicken liver: cook on the day you buy them. Fish pot leftovers are eaten the same day only.

            Shop

            New to this shop? How to pick, and what the names mean
            • Names: the grey words under an item are its common Hindi name, so you can ask for it at a shop.
            • Leafy greens: choose fresh, firm, green leaves, with no yellow patches and nothing slimy. The 750 g is the weight at the shop. After you trim off thick stems you will have about 500 g of leaves.
            • Cabbage, cauliflower, radish: pick ones that feel heavy for their size, with no black spots. Radish should be firm, with fresh leaves if you want to cook those too.
            • Fish: look for clear eyes, red gills and firm flesh, and it should not smell strongly. Ask the seller to clean it and cut it into pieces.
            • Eggs: clean shells with no cracks.
            • Curd: buy plain, unsweetened curd, and check the date.
            • Tomatoes and fruit: buy firm ones you can eat within a few days. If the tomatoes are poor, the arrow on that item suggests what to do.

            These are the rules the whole plan is built on. Each one says what to do and why, in plain words.

            Every day

            • Oil: 2 tbsp in total. 1 tbsp of mustard oil goes in the pot when you cook. 1 tbsp of olive oil goes on raw: 1 tsp on each bowl and ½ tsp on each salad. Use a proper measuring spoon until you can judge it by eye, because oil is easy to overpour.
            • Nuts: 30 g. That is 20 g of peanuts (a small handful, about 2 level tbsp) and 3 to 4 walnut halves.
            • Ground flaxseed: 1 to 2 tbsp. Always ground, never whole, because whole seeds pass straight through you. Never more than 2 tbsp a day.
            • Tea and coffee: 2 to 4 cups, with no sugar, an hour away from meals. Never have them with a lentil bowl, because they make it harder for your body to absorb the iron.
            • Lemon or gooseberry on every lentil bowl. The vitamin C in them helps your body take up the iron in the lentils.
            • Salt: iodised. Use ordinary iodised salt so that you get iodine. Black salt is a flavouring, not a replacement.

            Every week

            • Fish twice. The Blue Pot on Wednesday and Saturday covers it. It is steamed in the pot, never deep-fried. Count your fish meals on the Today tab.
            • Chicken liver once at most, 75 to 100 g, and it is optional. Buy it and cook it the same day.
            • Spinach now and then, but not as one of your two weekly leafy greens. Pick two other kinds for those.

            Safety

            • Kidney beans: soak for 10 hours, throw away the soaking water, then pressure-cook until a bean mashes between two fingers. Never eat them firm: undercooked kidney beans can make you ill.
            • Raw vegetables: rub them under running water; wash greens in 3 changes of water. Be extra careful in the monsoon.
            • Chicken and liver: cut one piece open. There must be no pink inside.
            • Peanuts: throw out any that look mouldy, shrivelled or taste bitter.
            • Mustard oil: buy it in a sealed bottle, heat it only until the first wisp of smoke, and never reuse it.
            • Second bowl: into the fridge within an hour, and reheat until it is steaming all the way through.
            • Cooker: never more than half full. After cooking, let the pressure drop by itself before you open it.
            • Pickle and fermented drink: always use a dry, clean spoon. Fuzzy mould means throw the whole jar out.

            Supplements and checks

            • Creatine monohydrate: 3 to 5 g a day. It is a plain white powder that you stir into a glass of water. Take it daily. B12 and D3 supplements only if a blood test shows you are low. Algae omega-3 is optional.
            • Blood test now, and again at 3 months. Ask for: B12 and vitamin D (nutrients that are easy to run low on), HbA1c (your average blood sugar over about 3 months), a lipid profile (cholesterol), hs-CRP (a sign of inflammation), liver enzymes, and CBC (a full blood count). If a kidney panel is included, tell the doctor that you take creatine; it raises the creatinine number a little.
            • Waist: under 90 cm. Measure with a soft tape at the navel, standing relaxed, snug but not squeezing. Do it on the same morning each week.

            Some words used below. kcal is what most people call calories: the energy in food. BMI compares weight to height. Resting burn is what your body uses just staying alive. A deficit means eating a little less than you burn, which is how weight comes down slowly. Protein is what protects and builds muscle.

            If the scale doesn’t move

            • Weigh on the same two mornings each week. Judge the 3-week trend and the waist, not single days.
            • Under 0.25 kg a week for 3 weeks: cut the grain in the pot from 60 g to 40 g (about 70 kcal). Still stuck: have the snack every other day.
            • Over 0.8 kg a week, or workouts getting weaker: put 30 g of grain back, or add a 4th egg.
            • Don’t go under about 1,800 kcal.

            Fibre: start gently

            This plan carries roughly 60 to 75 g of fibre a day, about double the usual advice. If you are coming from much less, expect gas for the first two weeks.

            • Week 1: 25 g soya chunks, 100 g of the cabbage-family vegetable, the roasted-gram drink every other day.
            • Week 2 onward: full amounts.
            • Drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water. The asafoetida, ginger and cumin in the pot help.
